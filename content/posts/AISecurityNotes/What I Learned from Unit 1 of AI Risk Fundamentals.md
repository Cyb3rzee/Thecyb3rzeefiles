About two weeks ago, I started AI Risk Fundamentals at Lens Academy. I've now completed Unit 1, and some of the ideas have got me thinking more about how AI works and the risks that could come with it.

The course is based on the book _If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies why superhuman ai would kill us all_. Here are four ideas that stood out to me.

## 1. Some predictions are easier than others

One idea from the introduction is that we can sometimes be confident about an outcome without knowing exactly how or when it will happen.

Think about an ice cube. We might not know exactly how long it will take to melt, but we know what will happen under the right conditions.

This got me thinking about AI risk and how we can reason about possible outcomes even when we don't know every detail of how they will happen.

## 2. What does intelligence mean?

The first chapter looks at intelligence as the ability to predict the world and steer it towards a goal.

Machines could eventually surpass humans in these abilities because they can operate at high speeds, copy information and capabilities, and potentially help improve future systems.

It made me think about how quickly AI capabilities could grow and what that might mean for our ability to control them.

## 3. AI is grown, not crafted

This was one of the ideas that stood out to me most.

Modern AI models are trained rather than programmed line by line. Engineers design the training process, but they cannot simply inspect a finished model and fully understand everything it has learned.

Another point is that an AI system behaving safely does not automatically mean it is safe. What happens when the system encounters a situation it was not trained or tested for?

That is one reason I find AI security interesting. We need to look beyond how a system behaves in familiar situations and consider what could happen when things go wrong.

## 4. Learning to want

The third chapter explores how training an AI system to achieve a goal can produce behaviours that help it reach that goal, including working around obstacles.

Nobody has to explicitly program every step of that behaviour. It can emerge through training.

This does not mean the AI has feelings or consciously wants something. The concern is about the behaviour it might develop while trying to achieve its objective.

## The part I enjoyed most

Apart from the course material, I really enjoyed our group discussion meeting.

I got to meet other people, share ideas and listen to different perspectives. I enjoyed discussing what we were learning instead of just going through the material on my own.

Sometimes, hearing how someone else understands an idea makes you look at it differently. That was one of my favourite parts of Unit 1.

## Moving on to Unit 2

Completing this first unit has given me more to think about as I continue learning about AI safety and security.

I'm looking forward to the next unit, especially the opportunity to understand these risks better and discuss them with other people.
